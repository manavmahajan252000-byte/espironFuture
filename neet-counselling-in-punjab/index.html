NEET counselling in Punjab: how MBBS and BDS seats are allotted

Last updated 21 November 2026. Written for students and parents in Amritsar and Punjab.

Qualifying NEET UG is only the first step. Seats are then filled through counselling, and Punjab students have two doors: the national MCC counselling and Punjab's own state counselling. This guide explains both, the steps, the documents and the mistakes that cost students seats.

A Punjab domicile student can take part in both counselling processes at the same time, which gives more seat options. Each process has its own registration, fees and deadlines.

How Punjab state counselling works

  • BFUHS runs the whole process online at bfuhs.ac.in for MBBS and BDS.
  • The 85% state quota is for Punjab domicile candidates only. You need a valid Punjab domicile certificate.
  • Seat numbers change every year, so check the seat matrix in the current BFUHS notice.
  • The merit list is built from your NEET score and your category.
  • You must have qualified NEET UG and meet the Class 12 marks rule in Physics, Chemistry and Biology.
Important

Never apply for state quota seats in two states. Counselling rules allow state quota seats in only one state, and breaking this rule can cancel your candidature.

The steps from registration to joining

  1. Register online on the official portal with your NEET roll number. MCC and BFUHS are separate registrations.
  2. Upload documents and pay the fee.
  3. Verification and merit list. Your documents are checked and the merit list is published. Check it carefully and use the objection window if something is wrong.
  4. Choice filling and locking. List colleges and courses in order of preference, then lock your choices before the deadline.
  5. Seat allotment. The result depends on your rank, your choices and your category.
  6. Reporting. Go to the allotted college with your original documents, and pay the fee through the bank challan or the method given. If you miss reporting, you can lose the seat.

Documents to keep ready

  • NEET UG admit card and scorecard
  • Class 10 and Class 12 marksheets and certificates
  • Punjab domicile certificate, for state quota seats
  • Category certificate, if you claim a reservation
  • Identity proof and passport-size photographs
  • Any other document listed in the current prospectus

Get missing certificates early. A domicile or category certificate can take weeks to arrange, and counselling deadlines are short.

Rounds and timing

MCC runs about four rounds for the All India Quota, plus stray vacancy rounds. Punjab usually has Round 1, Round 2, a mop-up round and a stray vacancy round.

Dates change every year. In 2025, Punjab registration opened in mid-July. In 2026, the state merit list came out in mid-August. Watch bfuhs.ac.in and mcc.nic.in, and not social media, for the real schedule.

MCC charges a registration fee and a refundable security deposit. The deposit for deemed universities is much larger than for other seats. Read the current bulletin for the amounts before you register.

Mistakes that cost students seats

  • Missing a registration, choice-locking or reporting deadline
  • Applying for state quota seats in more than one state
  • Arriving without original documents, or with a wrong domicile or category certificate
  • Filling choices in a hurry, or leaving choices unlocked. Unlocked choices may not be counted
  • Relying on rumours about cut-offs instead of the official seat matrix and earlier allotment lists

Medical colleges in Punjab

Examples of institutions with MBBS seats include AIIMS Bathinda, Government Medical College Amritsar, Government Medical College Patiala, Guru Gobind Singh Medical College Faridkot, and several private colleges. AIIMS Bathinda is filled through MCC, and state seats are filled through BFUHS. Check the current seat matrix for the full list.

Look at fees, approvals, hospital patient load and faculty before you rank private colleges. Ask us if you want help comparing them.

If you do not get a seat

Plan this before results, not after. Options include:

  • BDS, AYUSH (BAMS, BHMS) and BSc Nursing, which also use NEET
  • Allied health courses such as physiotherapy and medical lab technology
  • Biotechnology, microbiology, pharmacy and other life science degrees
  • A repeat year, if you and your family agree on a clear limit
  • MBBS abroad, only through colleges that meet NMC rules. See our guide to studying abroad after 12th

Common questions

Do I need a Punjab domicile certificate?

For state quota seats in government colleges, yes. All India Quota seats through MCC are not tied to your state. Some private management seats are open to candidates from other states.

Can I take part in both MCC and BFUHS counselling?

Yes, if you are eligible for both. They are separate processes with separate registrations and fees.

What NEET score do I need for MBBS in Punjab?

There is no fixed number. Cut-offs change every year with paper difficulty and the number of candidates. Look at the official allotment lists of previous years, and ask us for a realistic shortlist.

Is NEET needed for BDS, AYUSH and Nursing?

BDS, AYUSH and BSc Nursing seats are filled through NEET counselling. Other allied health courses use different routes.

What if I miss a counselling deadline?

You may lose your place in that round. Later rounds can help, but fewer seats remain, so put every deadline in your phone calendar.
