Study in the USA after 12th: what Indian students should know now

Last updated 21 November 2026. Written for students and parents in India.

The USA lets you apply directly to a four-year bachelor's degree after Class 12, and you can often decide your major after the first year or two. It is also one of the most expensive routes, and the visa rules changed in 2026. This guide shows how it works and what to check first.

What is changing

  • Test rules are tightening. A 2026 report says several top universities, including Harvard, Stanford, Cornell, MIT, Brown, Dartmouth, Yale, UPenn, Georgetown and Caltech, have ended test-optional policies. Others, such as NYU, remain test-optional for 2026 to 2027. Check each university.
  • Fewer Indian undergraduate applicants. A Common App report covering 1,100 institutions showed a 15% drop in Indian students seeking undergraduate admission for 2026 to 2027, while overall applications rose.
  • How long you may stay is changing. Reports say the US Department of Homeland Security formalised a rule ending the flexible duration of status system for F-1 students. It takes effect on 15 September 2026, sets fixed admission periods of up to four years, and shortens the grace period after finishing to 30 days. Students may need to apply for an extension if the course runs longer.

Policy on work after study is also under discussion. Check the latest on the official government sites before you rely on OPT in your plan.

How to apply after 12th

  1. Start early. Plan 12 to 18 months before the start date. Applications for the autumn intake usually open the year before.
  2. Build a profile: strong Class 11 and 12 marks, an SAT or ACT score where needed, an English test score, activities and projects.
  3. Write the essays yourself. In test-optional cycles the written parts carry a lot of weight.
  4. Choose a spread of universities: a few ambitious, a few realistic and at least two you are sure your family can afford.
  5. Wait for the I-20, the form the university sends you, before booking a visa interview.
Espiron tip

Many US universities offer little or no financial aid to international students. Ask each one directly whether aid is open to you before you assume it is.

What it costs

The total is tuition plus housing, food, insurance, books and travel, for every year. Each university publishes a cost of attendance for international students. Use that figure, not a headline tuition number.

  • Multiply the annual figure by four years and add a margin for exchange-rate changes
  • Part-time work is limited, so do not plan to fund tuition from it
  • Compare this with a public university in India or Germany so you see what the extra cost buys

Visa, work and stay

  • You need an F-1 visa and an interview at a US consulate. Prepare your finances and course plan so you can explain them clearly
  • On-campus work is limited to 20 hours a week during term, according to a 2026 summary
  • CPT must be part of your course. Twelve months of full-time CPT makes you ineligible for OPT, the same summary notes
  • STEM OPT gives an extra 24 months for eligible degrees, but government rules on OPT may change

Who it suits

The USA suits students who want a flexible four-year degree, have strong marks and profile, and whose family can fund the full cost with a clear Plan B. If the budget only just covers the first year, think carefully before you apply.

Common questions

Can I go to the USA directly after 12th?

Yes. Indian students can apply directly to four-year bachelor's programmes after Class 12. Each university sets its own marks, tests and documents.

Is the SAT compulsory?

Not everywhere. Many universities are test-optional, but several top ones now require scores. Check each university's own admissions page.

How much work can an international student do?

On-campus work is limited to 20 hours a week during term. Work off campus is only allowed under specific schemes such as CPT and OPT.

Is OPT guaranteed?

No. OPT is a work authorisation under current rules, and the rules can change. Do not make your financial plan depend on it.

Is a US degree worth the cost?

It can be, for the right course and student, but outcomes vary widely and the cost is high. Compare total cost with realistic first jobs, and keep a Plan B.
