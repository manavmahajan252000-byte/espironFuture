Studying abroad after 12th: basic information

Last updated 21 November 2026. Written for students and parents in Amritsar and Punjab.

Studying abroad can be a good choice, but it is expensive and rules change often. This page gives basic information only. We do not arrange visas, admissions or travel. Your questions about courses, costs and options are discussed one to one in a counselling session.

Is studying abroad right for you?

Before you start, ask three honest questions.

  • Can your family afford the full course, including living costs, insurance and travel, for every year?
  • Does a degree abroad give you something an Indian degree cannot, such as a specific course or research opportunity?
  • Are you ready to live far from home, manage money and handle setbacks?
Espiron tip

Treat studying abroad as a decision about the whole course, not just the first year. Ask for the total cost and a Plan B in India before you apply.

Steps and timeline

  1. Choose a course first, then countries and universities that offer it well.
  2. Check entry requirements on each university's own website: marks, subjects and tests.
  3. Prepare tests such as IELTS, TOEFL, PTE and, for some countries, SAT or ACT.
  4. Apply. Some countries use a central application service. Most others take direct applications, often with essays and references.
  5. Plan funds, including proof of funds that the visa requires.
  6. Apply for the visa only after you have an offer and the required documents.

Start 12 to 18 months before your intended start date. Applications for the autumn intake often open the year before.

Costs and funding

Add up four things for every year: tuition, living costs, health insurance and travel. Then compare the total with what your family can fund by savings, an education loan and scholarships.

  • Visa rules in several countries now ask for higher proof of funds, so you must show the money in the right form.
  • Part-time work is limited and should not be counted on to pay fees.
  • Large scholarships are competitive, and many big government scholarships are for postgraduate study. Many undergraduate scholarships come from the universities themselves, so ask each one.
  • Read the terms of any education loan, including interest, repayment start and what happens if you do not finish.

Studying medicine abroad

Many families look at MBBS abroad. Be careful and check the rules first.

  • Indian students must qualify NEET UG to take admission abroad if they want to practise medicine in India. A foreign university may admit you without NEET, but the degree will not help you register in India.
  • Foreign medical graduates must also pass a licensing exam in India. This is currently FMGE, which is planned to be replaced by NExT. Pass rates for FMGE have been low in recent years, reported at about one in four candidates in 2024.
  • The National Medical Commission has issued repeated advisories warning students against foreign colleges that do not meet its regulations.
  • Minimum course length and internship rules apply, so check that the college and country meet NMC requirements.

Avoiding scams and bad advice

  • No agent can guarantee a visa or a seat. Walk away from anyone who does.
  • Verify every offer letter on the university's own website or by contacting its admissions office.
  • Do not pay large fees to agents before you have a written offer. Get a written receipt for every payment.
  • Apply for your visa yourself or through a clearly authorised route, and read every form before you sign.
  • Be careful with promises of work or permanent residence. They depend on future rules and your own results.

Comparing with a degree in India

A good degree in India can cost far less and keep you close to family. Studying abroad can add exposure and a wider choice of courses, but it adds cost and risk. If your family is deciding between the two, compare the total cost, the course quality, the work-study rules, and the realistic job market at the end.

Common questions

Which country is best for Indian students?

There is no single best country. It depends on your course, budget, grades, visa rules and what you want after the degree. Compare two or three options on total cost and course quality.

Do I need SAT to study abroad?

Only some universities ask for it. Others ask for school results and an English test instead. Check each university's own page.

Is IELTS compulsory?

Most English-speaking countries ask for proof of English. IELTS is common, and some universities accept TOEFL, PTE or other tests. Check which tests your target universities and visa accept.

Can I work to pay my fees?

Student visas usually allow limited part-time work. The hours are capped, and wages are rarely enough to pay tuition, so you must plan your funds separately.

Is MBBS abroad a safe choice?

It can work, but it carries real risks. NEET is mandatory if you want to practise in India, and licensing exam pass rates have been low. Choose only colleges that meet NMC rules.
